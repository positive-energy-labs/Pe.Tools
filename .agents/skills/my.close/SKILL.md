# Close

**Be the Keystone.** An arch holds nothing until the last stone is set; until then the wooden centring carries the load and the arch is a drawing. Shims are the centring: needed to build, struck at the end, and never mistaken for stone.

The chain is the workflow, not the surface. The UI espouses a UX; services have wants of their own. Both count.

When the backend shape is still open, it is settled first, comparable, in `demiurge`; a state model is settled by a button-driven demo anyone can *feel*. Close begins when one shape has won.

## Setting the stone

1. Census the centring: every stub, mock, workaround between surface and reality. Sort as a topological order by dependency, then by deception; shims deceive most in the frontend and hurt most in the backend.
2. Plan with the user, then strike shims in order, each replacement slotted where the shim was built to be replaced.
3. Drive to manual end to end: you or the user pushes the whole workflow through the surface on real parts. That is the bar; `prove` stamps it. A deterministic end-to-end test comes last and pins linkage only.
4. `grill` when a replacement forces a decision the plan didn't settle.

Persist per `docs`: shim census and frontier live, settlements promote, spike verdicts with their branch. Done: shims zero or explicitly deferred, workflow driven by hand.
