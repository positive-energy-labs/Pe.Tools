How to Enter a Project

Here's a general overview of the steps to take in entering a project.  For more details on any one step, click the links within the text below.

1)Start a new project.  There are several ways to do that.

a)One way is to click the menu "File | New Project," then select another project from which to copy default data on the New Project dialog.

b)Another way is to click "File | Open Project," select a default project that you have created in the past on the Open Project dialog, and then save that project as a different filename  by clicking "File | Save Project As" and entering a new name on the Save Project As dialog.

c)Yet another way is to first create a default project and save it, then enter that project in the "Copy this file" input on the New Projects tab of the Rhvac Options dialog.  After doing that, then click the menu "File | New Project," and your new project will be a copy of your default project.

2)Save your new project.  This step is important because it makes it so if your computer crashes you will be able to recover the project data you have entered.  If you are working in the Untitled project and your computer crashes you will lose all of your work.

3)Enter all of  your General Project Data.

4)Open the System Data window and enter most of the data on this window.  You will need to revisit this window later, but for now you can enter data into most of the inputs.  Here are the things you should enter:

a)All of the inputs on the left hand side except for Ventilation and Exhaust (you will need to first enter all of your rooms in order to see the ASHRAE 62.2 requirement for ventilation.).

b)All of the inputs on the right hand side except for System CFM and Duct Load Factors (again, you will need to first enter all of your rooms before dealing with these inputs).  If you are not just using the program to simply tell you the heating and cooling loads on the house, and you will be selecting equipment or doing duct sizing, note that you will leave the System Air Type set to Auto at first, then after you have entered your project and calculated the loads, you will select equipment, change Auto to Fixed, and enter your equipment's CFM into the System CFM input.

5)If you are going to be entering the house as a block load (only one room) and you will not be using Drawing Board to enter your rooms, then you can skip this step.  Otherwise, open the Default Room Data window, and enter the following things:

a)Room height.  Enter the most common room height in your project.  You can leave every other input along the top of the window as is.

b)Floor material.  Enter the most common floor material in your project.  You can leave the width, length and other inputs at zero.

c)Roof material.  Enter the most common roof that occurs in your project.  Don't worry about the width, length and other inputs.

d)Wall material and height.  Again, enter the most common material.  Also enter the most common wall height, but you don't need to enter the length or other inputs.

e)Glass material, width, height and maybe overhang projection and offset.  Enter the most common window that will occur in the building, including the material, width and height.  If there will be an overhang that is the same for many windows you can also enter the overhang projection and offset.  Otherwise, leave them at zero.

f)Door material, width and height.  Enter the most common external door material and its dimensions.  Leave everything else blank.

6)If you are using Drawing Board to enter your rooms, open the Drawing Board window and create all of your rooms.  If the project is not yet in Drawing Board mode, first open the Room Data Entry Mode dialog and change the mode to Drawing Board.

7)If you are not using Drawing Board, open the Room Data window and enter all of your rooms.

8)There are three kinds of duct sizing that Rhvac can do  for you, as explained in the Duct Sizing Overview topic.  If you do not need any duct sizes, or if you only need the program to tell you the sizes of the main trunk and the runouts, then the built-in sizing is all you need.  But if you want to enter a complete duct system and calculate everything, including pressure loss information, then at this point you have the following options.

a)If you have entered your rooms with Drawing Board, you can either draw your duct system using the graphic Manual D Ductsize objects, or you can use the Tabular Manual D Ductsize window to enter your ductwork.

b)If you are not using Drawing Board, and you entered your rooms on the Room Data window, then you can use the Tabular Manual D Ductsize window to enter your duct system.

9)Return to the System Data window.  Now that you have entered all of your rooms, you can click the button beside the Ventilation input and see the ASHRAE 62.2 requirement for ventilation..  Note that you will not necessarily be entering the value Manual J recommends for ventilation, since there are several factors that determine what to do (for most houses zero ventilation is used; in some local jurisdictions, there are regulations about how much ventilation must be used; etc.).

10)  On the System Data  window you can now enter your Duct Load Factors information.  Click the button beside the Duct Load Factors input and then enter the Location and other information for your duct system.  If there are no ducts or if they are all in a conditioned space, then set the Location inputs to "Conditioned Space," click OK, and skip the rest of this step.  If you have entered a complete duct system using graphic Manual D Ductsize, then set the Update SA from [T]MDD input to MDD.  Or if you have entered a complete duct system using the Tabular Manual D Ductsize window enter TMDD for that input.  If you have not entered a complete duct system, then leave that input at No, and enter an appropriate surface area in the Surface Area input.  It is recommended that you hand calculate your duct surface area total by using these formulas.  To instead use only a rough estimate of the total duct surface area, use the popup Duct Surface Area Calculator dialog by clicking the item in the dropdown help for the Surface Area inputs.

11)  Open the Load Preview window and see what the sensible gain, latent gain, sensible loss, and system CFM results are for your system.  If you are using the program only to tell you the loads, and you are not doing duct sizing or selecting equipment, then skip the next several steps and go right to  selecting reports.  Otherwise, use the results on the Load Preview window to select your heating and cooling equipment.  We recommend that you obtain a copy of ACCA's Manual S publication and follow its guidelines on selecting equipment.  We also recommend that you obtain your manufacturer's performance data tables for the equipment you have in mind, and use those tables to select appropriately sized heating and cooling equipment for your project.

12)  Once you have chosen the equipment for your project, you can enter the model number and other data on the Equipment tab of the System Data window.  You can probably find your equipment in one of our downloadable equipment databases (click the menu "Help | Check for HVAC Equipment Update"), and automatically select it on the Find HVAC Equipment window.  That way you won't have to type everything in.

13)  After choosing your equipment, return to the Design tab of the System Data window, change the System Air Type input to Fixed, and enter your system's airflow in the System CFM input.

14)  Open the Load Preview window again (or click its Refresh button if it was already open), and check the loads again.  The loads may now be slightly different after you have entered a value for System CFM (due to a change in the duct loads).  Make sure that the equipment you selected is still appropriate for the job.

15)  Click the menu "File | Select Reports," choose the reports you want to include on the Select Reports dialog, and click the Preview button.  When you are ready to print your reports, click the Print All button in the Print Preview window's toolbar.
