- 2026-11-11, implementor default, re-openable by kaitpw (kernel crusade): a tab holds one long-lived socket, whatever it shows. Every push is a Reading kind on `/pe/resources`: `publication` (a host read's refresh-needed), `thread` with its own `after` cursor on the key, `notices`, and `page-target` (the bind chip). `/pe/read-events` and the web's `openEventSource` export are deleted, and the stream closes on `pagehide` and reopens on `pageshow`. Why: the host is HTTP/1.1, so Chrome gives the origin 6 sockets across every tab and back/forward-cached page; /ducts held 5 (one per host read), and the census subscription behind "Finding the open document" starved first (diag/doc-find 922a467c). Measured in Chrome 157 on the real transport bundle against an HTTP/1.1 stand-in host, before to after: a second tab's census subscription beside one /ducts tab, never in 8 s to 259 ms; the sockets of a page navigated away from, 60,053 ms to 35 ms; a page restored from the back/forward cache reopens its stream in 12 ms. `route-primitive.guard.test.ts` counts stream openers by call site and allows one. Two behaviours changed with the transport: a host read no longer marks itself stale while the stream is down (it reads again when the host answers `refresh-needed`), and the thread view no longer says "Thread stream dropped" during an outage (the Readings owner reports the fault and `onResync` re-reads the body).
